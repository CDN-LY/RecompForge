Calculator Accuracy and Limitations

The RecompForge calculator uses established scientific formulas including the Mifflin-St Jeor equation for BMR estimation and validated activity multipliers for TDEE calculation. These formulas produce reasonable estimates for most healthy adults but are not perfectly precise for every individual.

Sources of Estimation Error

The calculator estimates body fat percentage using population-average assumptions based on gender. Actual body fat percentage can differ significantly between individuals with the same height, weight, and age. Lean body mass — which drives protein targets — is therefore also an estimate, not a measured value.

Activity multipliers are self-reported and subject to inaccuracy. Most people tend to overestimate their activity level, which can result in an overestimated TDEE. We recommend starting with a conservative activity multiplier and adjusting based on real-world weight and performance tracking over 3–4 weeks.

Calorie and macronutrient targets are starting points, not fixed prescriptions. They should be treated as an initial framework to be refined based on your individual response over time.

For Best Results

Use the calculator results as a starting estimate, then track your bodyweight and performance weekly. If your weight is not responding as expected after 3–4 weeks, adjust your calorie intake by 100–150 kcal in the appropriate direction rather than making large changes all at once.

Individual Results Vary

Any references to typical results, expected fat loss rates, or estimated muscle gain rates on this website are general population averages derived from scientific literature. Your actual results will depend on a wide range of factors that the calculator cannot account for, including your genetics and individual metabolic rate, your training history and current fitness level, your sleep quality and stress levels, your hormonal health, your consistency and adherence over time, and the accuracy of your calorie and food tracking.

RecompForge makes no guarantees about specific outcomes. The information provided is not a promise of results, and individual outcomes will vary considerably.

Seek Professional Advice

Before beginning any new diet or exercise programme, especially if you have a pre-existing medical condition, a history of disordered eating, cardiovascular concerns, diabetes, or any other health issue, you should seek the advice of a qualified professional.

Registered Dietitian

A registered dietitian can provide personalised nutrition advice tailored to your health status, medical history, food preferences, and specific goals. They can also help you identify and avoid nutritional deficiencies that may arise during periods of calorie restriction.

General Practitioner or Physician

Your doctor can advise whether a particular dietary approach is appropriate given your individual health profile, screen for underlying conditions that might affect your metabolism, and monitor health markers during significant body composition changes.

Certified Personal Trainer

A qualified personal trainer can design a safe and progressive resistance training programme suited to your current fitness level, help you execute exercises correctly to minimise injury risk, and adjust programming as your fitness improves.
